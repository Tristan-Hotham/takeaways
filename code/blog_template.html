Where is Britain's chip shop capital? Its curry capital? I sorted every takeaway and restaurant on the UK's food hygiene register by what its name says it sells, and counted them against the people who live nearby.

Every place in the UK that serves food to the public, from the chippy to the school canteen, is inspected by its council and given a food hygiene rating. The Food Standards Agency publishes the whole register as open data: {{n_all_types|int}} food businesses, each with a name, a kind of business, a postcode and, for most, a point on the map. Among them are {{n_T|int}} takeaways and sandwich shops and {{n_R|int}} restaurants, cafes and canteens.

The register tells you a place is a takeaway. It does not tell you what it sells. But the names usually do: Jubilee Fish Bar, Simla Tandoori, Happy Garden, Bosphorus Istanbul. So I wrote a set of simple, open rules that read each name and sort it into one of sixteen kinds of food, tested the rules on samples of individually labelled names, and put the results on a map of every postcode district.

Six maps of Britain's takeaways

Fish and chips is still the commonest takeaway with a name that says what it sells: {{classified.T.by_family.fish_chips|int}} of them, just ahead of pizza ({{classified.T.by_family.pizza|int}}) and Chinese ({{classified.T.by_family.chinese|int}}). It is the most common named takeaway in {{stories.lead_family_counts.fish_chips|int}} of the UK's {{n_counties|int}} counties and unitary authorities. But the six maps are strikingly different. Chinese takeaways are everywhere, and thickest in Scotland, Wales and the north. Kebabs and chicken shops are city foods, and above all London foods.

The chip shop capital is the seaside

The postcode district with the most chip shops per resident is Skegness, with {{named_districts.PE25.fish_chips|f1}} for every 100,000 people, then Bridlington ({{named_districts.YO15.fish_chips|f1}}) and Filey ({{named_districts.YO14.fish_chips|f1}}). These are resorts, and their chippies are feeding visitors as much as locals. Among council areas of 100,000 people or more, Blackpool wins with {{stories.fish_chips.county_top.0.rate|f1}} per 100,000, ahead of {{stories.fish_chips.county_top.1.name|raw}} and {{stories.fish_chips.county_top.2.name|raw}}. By nation, Wales has the most chip shops per head.

Curry in Hounslow, Chinatowns still on the map

The curry capital is Hounslow. By postcode it is TW3, in the middle of Hounslow, with {{named_districts.TW3.indian|f1}} Indian, Bangladeshi, Pakistani and Nepalese places per 100,000 residents, and Southall's UB1 ({{named_districts.UB1.indian|f1}}) is close behind. The Chinese map is the most even of the big foods, because Chinese takeaways spread to almost every town after the war. But its peaks are where they always were: Birmingham's Chinese Quarter (B5), Manchester's Chinatown (M1) and Liverpool's (L1) are the three postcode districts with most Chinese places per head.

Kebabs are a north London speciality: {{stories.kebab.county_top.0.name|raw}}, {{stories.kebab.county_top.1.name|raw}} and {{stories.kebab.county_top.2.name|raw}} top the list, the boroughs of north London's Turkish and Kurdish high streets. Every one of the top ten council areas for chicken shops is a London borough, led by {{stories.chicken.county_top.0.name|raw}}. Pizza is the odd one: it peaks around the Humber and Teesside, in {{stories.pizza.county_top.0.name|raw}} and {{stories.pizza.county_top.1.name|raw}}.

Four nations, four tastes

Scotland has the most takeaways per head of the four nations, {{nations.Scotland.T_all_100k|f1}} per 100,000 against {{nations.England.T_all_100k|f1}} in England, and the most curry houses. Wales leads on chip shops and kebabs. Northern Ireland, which I did not expect, has the most pizza places per head ({{nations.Northern Ireland.TR_pizza_100k|f1}} per 100,000). England has by far the most chicken shops.

Where takeaways outnumber restaurants

Across the UK there are {{stories.ratio.uk|f2}} takeaways for every restaurant or cafe on the register. In only {{headline.n_counties_T_gt_R|int}} council areas of 100,000 people or more are there more takeaways than restaurants: {{stories.ratio.county_top.0.name|raw}}, {{stories.ratio.county_top.1.name|raw}}, {{stories.ratio.county_top.2.name|raw}} and {{stories.ratio.county_top.3.name|raw}}. At the other end, {{stories.ratio.county_bottom.0.name|raw}} has {{stories.ratio.county_bottom.0.ratio|f2}} takeaways per restaurant. One caution: councils decide which places count as takeaways, and they do not all decide alike.

Chains and clean kitchens

Chip shops are the great independent trade: only {{chains.fish_chips.chain_n|int}} of {{chains.fish_chips.n_family|int}} carry a chain name I could recognise. Burgers are the opposite, with McDonald's alone {{chains.burger.McDonald's.share|f1}}% of burger places.

Takeaways rate lower for hygiene than restaurants: {{hygiene.by_group.T.pct5|f1}}% of rated takeaways have the top score of 5, against {{hygiene.by_group.R.pct5|f1}}% of restaurants and cafes. Sushi places do best ({{hygiene.by_family_T.japanese.pct5|f1}}% with a 5) and Indian takeaways worst ({{hygiene.by_family_T.indian.pct5|f1}}%). Chain takeaways score a 5 far more often than independents ({{hygiene.chain_vs_indep_T.chain.pct5|f1}}% against {{hygiene.chain_vs_indep_T.independent.pct5|f1}}%).

How I did this

I downloaded the whole register from the Food Standards Agency's ratings service on {{retrieved|raw}}. The name rules look first for chains, then for strong words (fish bar, chippy, tandoori, kebab, pizza, chinese), and only then for weaker hints (dragon, golden, spice). They gave a kind of food to {{classified.T.classified_pct|f1}}% of takeaways. To test them, I drew a fresh random sample of {{accuracy.B.all.n|int}} names after the rules were finished, each labelled individually by reading the name, as part of the AI-assisted analysis, and reviewed by the author: {{accuracy.B.all.precision_pct|f1}}% of the rules' answers were right. OpenStreetMap, whose volunteers tag many takeaways with a cuisine, agreed with the rules {{osm.agree_pct|f1}}% of the time across {{osm.n_both|int}} matched places.

Populations come from the 2021 Census for England, Wales and Northern Ireland and the 2022 Census for Scotland, by postcode district, and from the ONS mid-{{county_pop_year|raw}} estimates for counties. There are no official postcode district boundaries, so the district shapes on the maps are my own approximation, drawn from where each district's businesses are. A city centre or a resort has far more takeaways per head than its residents could eat, so on the interactive map you can also switch to the share of an area's places that sell each kind of food.
