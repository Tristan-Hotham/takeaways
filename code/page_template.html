Chips, curry or kebab?

Every takeaway and restaurant on the UK's food hygiene register, sorted by what its name says it sells, and mapped per head for every postcode district and county. Where is the chip shop capital? The curry capital? Where do takeaways outnumber restaurants?

Dr Tristan Hotham, Independent researcher · 2026 · Data: Food Standards Agency food hygiene ratings (Open Government Licence v3); ONS, NRS and NISRA populations; OpenStreetMap (ODbL) as a check

Britain's high streets keep a register. Every place that serves food to the public, from the chip shop to the school canteen, is inspected by its council and given a food hygiene rating, and the Food Standards Agency publishes the lot as open data: {{n_all_types|int}} food businesses across England, Wales, Northern Ireland and Scotland, each with its name, its kind of business, its postcode and, for most, a point on the map.

The register says whether a place is a takeaway or a restaurant. It does not say what it sells. But the names do: Jubilee Fish Bar, Simla Tandoori, Happy Garden, Bosphorus Istanbul. I sorted every takeaway, restaurant, cafe and mobile caterer by its name into sixteen kinds of food with a set of open, checkable rules, tested the rules on hundreds of individually labelled names, compared them with OpenStreetMap's cuisine tags, and counted them against the people who live in each postcode district and county.

Two cautions before reading it. First, a postcode district with few residents and a busy high street, a city centre or a seaside resort, will have far more takeaways per head than the people living there could ever eat; switch to "Share of places" to ask what a place's takeaways sell rather than how many there are. Second, about a third of takeaways have names that do not say what they sell (Tasty Bite, Munchies), so every count here is a floor, not a census.

    06 · Clean kitchensHygiene ratings by kind of food

    In England, Wales and Northern Ireland, councils rate hygiene from 0 (urgent improvement necessary) to 5 (very good); Scotland uses Pass or Improvement Required. Takeaways rate lower than restaurants: {{hygiene.by_group.T.pct5|f1}}% of rated takeaways have a 5, against {{hygiene.by_group.R.pct5|f1}}% of restaurants and cafes, {{hygiene.by_group.P.pct5|f1}}% of pubs and {{hygiene.by_group.M.pct5|f1}}% of mobile caterers. Among takeaways, sushi and Japanese places rate best ({{hygiene.by_family_T.japanese.pct5|f1}}% with a 5) and Indian takeaways lowest ({{hygiene.by_family_T.indian.pct5|f1}}%, with {{hygiene.by_family_T.indian.pct_le2|f1}}% rated 2 or below). Chain takeaways do much better than independents ({{hygiene.chain_vs_indep_T.chain.pct5|f1}}% against {{hygiene.chain_vs_indep_T.independent.pct5|f1}}% with a 5).

    07 · Checking the namesHow good is a guess from a name?

    The rules give a kind of food to {{classified.T.classified_pct|f1}}% of takeaways, {{classified.R.classified_pct|f1}}% of restaurants and cafes and {{classified.M.classified_pct|f1}}% of mobile caterers; the rest have names that do not say. To test them, I drew a fresh random sample of {{accuracy.B.all.n|int}} businesses after the rules were finished ({{accuracy.B.T.n|int}} takeaways and {{accuracy.B.R.n|int}} restaurants) and each name was labelled individually by reading the name, as part of the AI-assisted analysis, and reviewed by the author, without looking at the rules' answer. Of the names the rules sorted, {{accuracy.B.all.precision_pct|f1}}% were sorted right (95% interval {{accuracy.B.all.precision_ci.0|f1}} to {{accuracy.B.all.precision_ci.1|f1}}%). Of the names that do plainly say what they sell, the rules caught {{accuracy.B.all.recall_pct|f1}}%; the misses were names like Erawan (Thai) or Chettinad (south Indian) that need outside knowledge.

    OpenStreetMap is an independent check. Its volunteers tag many takeaways with a cuisine. I matched {{osm.n_matched|int}} OpenStreetMap takeaways and restaurants to the register by name and place (within 100 metres). Where both say what the food is, they agree {{osm.agree_pct|f1}}% of the time. OpenStreetMap also shows what the rules miss: of matched places the rules left unsorted but OpenStreetMap gives a cuisine, the most common are Chinese, Indian and Italian places with names that do not say so. And the two sources draw much the same map: across postcode districts the rank correlation between the two counts per head is {{osm.rho_district.chicken|f2}} for chicken shops, {{osm.rho_district.pizza|f2}} for pizza and {{osm.rho_district.fish_chips|f2}} for chip shops; lower than perfect because OpenStreetMap is patchier in some places than others.

    How I did this

    Data. The Food Standards Agency's ratings API (version 2), retrieved on {{retrieved|raw}}: every establishment of each of the fourteen business types, {{n_all_types|int}} in all, of which {{n_T|int}} takeaways and sandwich shops, {{n_R|int}} restaurants, cafes and canteens, {{n_P|int}} pubs, bars and nightclubs and {{n_M|int}} mobile caterers. Scotland's businesses are in the same register under the Food Hygiene Information Scheme. {{geocoded_pct|f1}}% of these businesses have a map point; the rest are counted through their postcode and council.

    Names. A set of keyword rules, in code/classify.py, reads each name: national chains first, then strong words (fish bar, chippy, tandoori, kebab, pizza, chinese), and only when nothing strong fires, weaker hints (dragon, golden, spice). A name with two strong families (Fish Bar and Chinese) goes to the first in a fixed order. Pubs are not sorted. The rules do not read menus, so a curry house called The Rose stays unsorted.

    People. Postcode districts: Census 2021 usual residents (England and Wales, ONS table P004; Northern Ireland, NISRA) and Scotland's Census 2022 postcode counts summed by district (NRS). Counties and unitary authorities: ONS mid-{{county_pop_year|raw}} population estimates, ONS boundaries of December 2023. Each business takes the county of its inspecting council, which sits inside one county or unitary authority.

    Areas. There are no official postcode district boundaries, so I drew my own: each 750 metre square of land takes the district most common among its nearest food businesses and Scottish census postcodes. Edges are approximate; the counts are not, because every business keeps its own postcode. {{n_districts_drawn|int}} of {{n_districts|int}} districts with residents are drawn; the rest are tiny central districts, counted but too small to show.

    Names as published. {{n_names_dash|int}} business names containing en or em dashes are written with plain hyphens in the published files.

    Limits. The register is a register of inspected premises, not of shops open today. Councils assign business types differently. Mobile caterers are registered where their owner is based, so they are left off the maps. A takeaway in a resort or a city centre serves visitors and workers as well as residents. Kitchens that only deliver ("dark kitchens") and several brands run from one kitchen appear as one or several entries depending on how they registered.
